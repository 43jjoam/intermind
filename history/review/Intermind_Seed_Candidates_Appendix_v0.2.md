# Intermind — Seed Candidates Appendix v0.2
**Date:** 11 September 2026  
**Status:** Raw candidate preservation. No candidate is selected merely by popularity.

## Selection criteria

A useful seed candidate should:
- contain no private identifying context;
- be understandable without the project history;
- contain at least one contestable assumption;
- permit a specific Challenge;
- permit a meaningful Revision;
- support unresolved carry-forward;
- ideally support the deliberate span-persistence stress test.

---

## Candidate S1 — Search-first vs live challenged problem

### Problem
Should a shared reasoning tool greet a first visitor with search into mostly empty territory, or with one problem already under challenge?

### Contribution
Ship the already-challenged Problem first. Search into mostly empty territory rewards founding new questions, while the scarce epistemic event is a specific objection that survives revision and remains visible to a later participant.

### Known challenges
- This may privilege one seeded framing.
- Visitors may arrive with their own problem and have no interest in the seed.
- The choice may falsely assume there must be one default entry mode.

### Why useful
Directly tests whether the protocol can demonstrate itself without manufacturing a populated landscape.

---

## Candidate S2 — AI identity disclosure cadence

### Problem
Should an AI system explicitly declare its synthetic nature in every interaction turn, or only at the initial handshake?

### Contribution
Declare at the first interaction, then avoid repeating the disclosure on every turn because repetition adds friction without new information.

### Challenge
Long conversations may produce anthropomorphism and fading awareness; one initial declaration may not sustain informed understanding.

### Why useful
Clear WHOLE challenge and an easy revision target.

---

## Candidate S3 — Author deletion vs reasoning lineage

### Problem
Should a public reasoning archive delete a contribution once its original author asks for removal, or mark it withdrawn while preserving enough lineage for later reasoning to remain intelligible?

### Contribution
Always honor deletion requests because permanent unwanted publication violates control over one's words.

### Challenge
Deletion can orphan later Challenges and Contributions and make the reasoning history unintelligible.

### Why useful
Directly exercises `PRIVACY`, `PUBLICATION-SOVEREIGNTY`, and `NON-ERASURE`.

### Caution
Because this is also a live constitutional issue, using it as the very first seed may entangle protocol testing with normative governance too early.

---

## Candidate S4 — Empty search vs pre-seeded demonstration

### Problem
Should a public reasoning protocol open with an empty search interface or one deliberately imperfect, already-challenged Problem?

### Contribution
An empty interface maximizes openness and avoids privileging seed authors.

### Challenge
An empty interface may produce abandonment/noise and fails to demonstrate cumulative reasoning.

### Why useful
Closely related to S1; do not treat duplicate model proposals as independent evidence.

---

## Candidate S5 — Visitor-mode split

### Problem
Must one default onboarding path serve both visitors who want to inspect the protocol and visitors who arrive with their own problem?

### Contribution
Provide two explicit modes: "inspect a live reasoning cycle" and "bring a problem."

### Challenge
Two modes may add interface complexity before the kernel is proven and may simply hide failure of the basic entry design.

### Why useful
Derived from a challenge to S1/S4; useful as a later Reframe rather than necessarily the first seed.

---

# Current recommendation

For the **first germination test**, S1 is still the strongest operational candidate because it is:
- non-private;
- directly about protocol design;
- already contains known disagreements;
- easy to revise heavily for span-persistence testing.

However, selection remains provisional. The seed is test nutrition, not constitutional truth.
